🔥 Key Signals

1. Demand is holding up, but travellers are actively reallocating where and how they spend.

Korean consumers remain willing to travel—80% expect to take at least as many overseas trips in 2027 and 68% plan to maintain or increase lodging spend—but 54% have already switched destinations because of exchange rates. India’s platform evidence points in the same direction through a different mechanism: Scapia says October–December bookings are three to four times last year, with heritage stays at 4.8 times and safari stays at 6.6 times. The leadership read is not ‘universal premiumisation’; it is resilient intent paired with sharper value comparison and experience-led substitution. [KOREA2 · Oct 14 (Wed)] [INDIA11 · Oct 18 (Sun)]

2. Public policy is being used to redistribute travel, not merely increase national totals.

Japan is concentrating support on regional destinations capable of attracting visitors who spend at least JPY1 million on the ground, while Taiwan is using airline, OTA and creator partnerships to steer international visitors south. These interventions make destination conversion an execution question: transport, bookable inventory, language support and a clear reason to stay must work together. 💡 Airbnb APAC Implications

1. Merchandise whole-home value around the occasion, not around generic accommodation.

Festive India demand is over-indexing to heritage and safari stays; Korea’s wellness data show Western yoga bookings roughly tripling and Japanese spa bookings rising about 2.4 times. Build destination pages around the group’s purpose—family celebration, nature, wellness or a high-involvement interest—and make sleeping configuration, kitchens, gathering space, cancellation terms and total price legible. Test completed nights and guest satisfaction by occasion; do not infer Airbnb share from category growth alone. [INDIA11 · Oct 18 (Sun)] [KOREA6 · Oct 16 (Fri)]

2. Use corridor-level operating plans for regional dispersal.

For southern Taiwan, regional Japan and non-capital Korea, pair demand generation with available legal supply, realistic transfers, multilingual check-in and local experiences. Start with a few origin–destination pairs where policy support and transport overlap, then measure search-to-book conversion, cancellations and incremental nights against nearby control destinations. National arrival growth is too broad to tell whether a specific secondary market can absorb demand without degrading reliability. Airbnb should benchmark the guest’s redeemable total basket and service confidence, then close preventable friction in price transparency, arrival instructions and issue resolution. Broad coupon matching would be premature without evidence of profitable incremental nights; distinctive space and group utility remain the stronger source of differentiation.
